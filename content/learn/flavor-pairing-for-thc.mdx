## The Flavor Challenge

Let's be honest: traditional cannabis edibles often taste... like cannabis. That "weedy" flavor can overwhelm a dish, which is why so many edibles hide behind chocolate or sugar.

Grown products are different. Our extraction and formulation process eliminates the strong cannabis taste. But understanding flavor pairing still matters—it helps you build dishes where THC oil and salt enhance rather than distract.

## THC Oil: Flavor Profile

Grown Heirloom Oil is built on a cold-pressed olive blend. The THC is nearly undetectable, leaving you with:

- Mild, buttery olive notes
- Slight peppery finish
- Clean, neutral fat carrier
- No bitterness or vegetal taste

**Think of it as:** A high-quality finishing olive oil with a secret superpower.

## THC Salt: Flavor Profile

Grown Flake Salt is made from harvested grey sea salt. You'll taste:

- Clean, bright salinity
- Subtle mineral complexity
- Pleasant crunch
- No cannabis flavor

**Think of it as:** The finishing salt you'd use anyway—but with benefits.

## Flavors That Complement THC Oil

### Natural Partners

These ingredients naturally pair well with olive oil and therefore work beautifully with THC oil:

**Citrus**
- Lemon, lime, orange, grapefruit
- Brightness cuts through richness
- Ideal for: dressings, marinades, drizzled fish

**Herbs**
- Basil, oregano, rosemary, thyme
- Mediterranean flavors love olive oil
- Ideal for: pasta, roasted vegetables, focaccia

**Garlic**
- Raw, roasted, or gently sautéed
- Classic olive oil companion
- Ideal for: pasta aglio e olio, bruschetta

**Cheese (especially aged)**
- Parmesan, pecorino, aged gouda
- Fat + umami = harmony
- Ideal for: pasta finishes, cheese plates

**Tomatoes**
- Fresh, roasted, or in sauce
- Acidic + fatty = balanced
- Ideal for: caprese, pasta, bruschetta

**Balsamic**
- Sweet-tart complexity
- Classic dipping partner
- Ideal for: bread service, salad dressing

### Flavor Bridges

These ingredients help integrate THC oil into unexpected dishes:

- **Honey** — Drizzled with oil on cheese or fruit
- **Chili flakes** — Heat masks any subtle notes
- **Nuts** — Toasted almonds, pine nuts, walnuts
- **Bitter greens** — Arugula, radicchio, endive

## Flavors That Complement THC Salt

### Sweet-Salty Combinations

THC salt shines brightest on sweet dishes where salt is a contrast:

**Chocolate**
- Brownies, cookies, truffles, mousse
- Salt makes chocolate taste more chocolatey
- Ideal for: dessert finishing

**Caramel**
- Sauces, candies, topped desserts
- Classic salted caramel upgrade
- Ideal for: ice cream, tarts, apples

**Fruit**
- Watermelon, mango, pineapple, berries
- Salt amplifies natural sweetness
- Ideal for: fresh fruit plates, fruit salads

### Savory Applications

**Eggs**
- Any style: fried, scrambled, poached
- Salt's natural home
- Ideal for: brunch dishes

**Avocado**
- Toast, guacamole, sliced
- Creamy + crunchy + salty
- Ideal for: toast, salads

**Tomatoes**
- Fresh sliced with oil and salt
- Simple perfection
- Ideal for: summer appetizers

**Grilled proteins**
- Steak, lamb, pork, fish
- Finishing salt's traditional role
- Ideal for: dinner entrées

## Building a THC Menu

When planning a meal with THC oil and salt, think about:

### Balance Across Courses

Don't overload one course. Distribute THC thoughtfully:

**Appetizer:** Light THC (if any) — 2-3mg per person max
**Main:** Primary THC dose — 5-10mg per person
**Dessert:** Optional THC salt — 2-4mg per person

### Flavor Progression

Build from lighter to heavier:
1. Start with bright, acidic flavors
2. Move to richer, fattier dishes
3. End with sweet or umami notes

### Textural Variety

THC salt adds crunch. Use it to contrast:
- Creamy soups
- Smooth chocolate
- Soft avocado
- Silky pasta

## What to Avoid

### Overpowering Flavors

Very strong flavors can fight with oil's subtle taste:
- Extremely spicy dishes (overwhelming)
- Very vinegary preparations (unbalanced)
- Heavy smoky flavors (competing)

### Confusing Contexts

Don't put THC in dishes where guests won't expect it:
- Bread basket (too easy to overeat)
- Drinks (variable consumption)
- Communal dips (hard to track doses)
