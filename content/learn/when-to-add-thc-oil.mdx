## The Timing Question

"When should I add THC oil?" is one of the most common questions we hear. The answer depends on what you're cooking, how hot it's getting, and what experience you want to create.

Here's the framework we use.

## The Three Timing Strategies

### 1. Finish (Recommended for Most Dishes)

**Add THC oil after cooking is complete.**

This is the safest and most reliable approach. No heat exposure means no potency loss, and you have complete control over distribution.

**Best for:**
- Pasta (toss with finished noodles)
- Salads (mix into dressing)
- Soups (drizzle on top)
- Roasted vegetables (drizzle after plating)
- Pizza (drizzle on finished slices)
- Rice and grain bowls

**How to do it:**
1. Complete your dish normally with regular cooking oil
2. Let the dish cool slightly (not steaming hot)
3. Add measured THC oil and toss/mix gently
4. Serve immediately

### 2. Low-Heat Cook

**Add THC oil during cooking at temperatures below 300°F.**

This works well for dishes that cook gently and never get screaming hot. The oil integrates into the dish while preserving THC.

**Best for:**
- Sautéed vegetables at medium heat
- Scrambled eggs (add oil to eggs, cook low and slow)
- Slow-simmered sauces (add at end, simmer gently)
- Warm vinaigrettes
- Melted butter sauces

**How to do it:**
1. Cook your dish at medium or medium-low heat
2. Add THC oil in the last 2-3 minutes of cooking
3. Stir gently to distribute
4. Remove from heat before oil gets too hot

### 3. No-Cook

**Never expose THC oil to heat.**

Some dishes are naturally cold or room temperature. These are ideal for THC oil because there's zero degradation.

**Best for:**
- Salad dressings
- Pestos and cold sauces
- Dips (hummus, baba ganoush)
- Compound butters (soft, not melted)
- Overnight marinades (for serving, not cooking)
- Smoothies and drinks

## What to Avoid

### High Heat = Lost Potency

Avoid using THC oil for:
- **Searing** (400°F+ pan temperatures)
- **Deep frying** (350-375°F sustained heat)
- **Roasting** at high temps (oil would burn anyway)
- **Grilling** (direct flame)
- **Baking** where oil is in the batter

### Extended Heat Exposure

Even moderate heat can degrade THC if exposure is prolonged:
- Long braises or stews (unless added at the very end)
- Slow cooker recipes
- Canning or preserving

## Temperature Guide

| Temperature | THC Impact | Recommendation |
|-------------|------------|----------------|
| Below 250°F | Minimal degradation | Safe for gentle cooking |
| 250-320°F | Some degradation | Okay for brief exposure |
| 320-350°F | Noticeable degradation | Add after cooking |
| Above 350°F | Significant loss | Never use THC oil |

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Garlic Pasta

**Traditional method:**
1. Cook pasta, reserve pasta water
2. Sauté garlic in regular olive oil
3. Toss pasta with garlic oil
4. **Add THC oil now**, off heat
5. Add pasta water, cheese, plate

### Example 2: Roasted Vegetables

**Traditional method:**
1. Toss vegetables with regular oil, salt, pepper
2. Roast at 400°F until caramelized
3. Remove from oven, let cool 2 minutes
4. **Drizzle THC oil** over finished vegetables
5. Toss gently, serve

### Example 3: Vinaigrette

**No heat needed:**
1. Combine THC oil, regular olive oil, vinegar
2. Add mustard, herbs, salt, pepper
3. Shake or whisk to emulsify
4. Store in fridge, use cold

## Distributing Oil Evenly

THC oil tends to pool or stick to surfaces. For even distribution:

1. **Measure carefully** before adding
2. **Toss or stir thoroughly** to coat all surfaces
3. **Divide portions first** if serving multiple people
4. **Drizzle in a circular motion** for plated dishes
5. **Emulsify with vinegar/citrus** for dressings

## When Precision Matters Most

For dinner parties and shared meals:
- Calculate total THC, divide by servings
- Add oil to the whole batch, not individual plates
- Mix thoroughly before portioning
- Double-check your math
