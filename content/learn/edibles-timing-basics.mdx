## Why Timing Matters

Edibles are not like smoking or vaping. The effects take longer to begin, last longer, and feel different. Understanding this timing is essential for:

- Planning meals and social events
- Avoiding overconsumption
- Creating comfortable experiences for yourself and guests

## The Edibles Timeline

### Onset: When Effects Begin

**Oil-based edibles (like Grown Oil):**
- First effects: 30-90 minutes
- Typical: 45-60 minutes

**THC salt (sublingual absorption):**
- First effects: 15-30 minutes
- Faster because some THC absorbs through mouth tissues

**What affects onset:**
- Empty vs. full stomach (empty = faster)
- Individual metabolism
- Body weight and composition
- Tolerance level
- Fat content of the meal

### Peak: When Effects Are Strongest

**For most edibles:**
- Peak effects: 2-3 hours after consumption
- This is when you'll feel the strongest

**For THC salt:**
- Peak may occur earlier (1-2 hours)
- May be less intense due to sublingual absorption

### Duration: How Long Effects Last

**Total duration:**
- Light dose (2.5-5mg): 4-6 hours
- Moderate dose (5-15mg): 6-8 hours
- Higher dose (15mg+): 8-12 hours

Effects gradually taper—you won't feel the peak for the entire duration.

## Planning Your Evening

### Dinner Party Timeline

Here's how to structure an evening with THC-infused food:

**7:00 PM** — Guests arrive, serve non-infused appetizers
**7:30 PM** — Serve THC-infused first course (if applicable)
**8:00 PM** — Main course (with THC option clearly marked)
**8:45 PM** — First course effects beginning for early course
**9:00 PM** — Dessert (with optional THC salt)
**10:00 PM** — Peak effects from dinner course
**11:00 PM** — Wind-down, ensure guests have safe transport home

### Key Rules

1. **Never send guests home at peak** — Plan for effects to taper before departure
2. **Pace the courses** — Don't stack multiple THC dishes back-to-back
3. **Offer non-infused alternatives** — Every course should have an option
4. **Disclose clearly** — Announce THC content before serving

## The Mistake Everyone Makes

**Eating more because "it's not working."**

This is the #1 cause of uncomfortable edibles experiences. Here's why it happens:

1. You eat an infused dish at 7:30 PM
2. By 8:15 PM, you feel nothing
3. You think "this isn't working" and eat more
4. By 9:00 PM, the first dose kicks in
5. By 9:30 PM, the second dose kicks in
6. By 10:00 PM, you're uncomfortably high

**The solution:** Wait at least 90 minutes—ideally 2 hours—before deciding if you need more.

## Oil vs. Salt: Timing Differences

| Factor | THC Oil | THC Salt |
|--------|---------|----------|
| Onset | 45-90 minutes | 15-30 minutes |
| Peak | 2-3 hours | 1-2 hours |
| Duration | 6-8 hours | 4-6 hours |
| Intensity | Can be stronger | Often more subtle |

THC salt's faster onset comes from sublingual absorption—THC enters your bloodstream through mouth tissues rather than passing through digestion.

## Factors That Speed Up Onset

- Eating on an empty stomach
- Consuming with fatty foods (helps absorption)
- Higher metabolism
- Previous cannabis tolerance
- THC salt instead of oil

## Factors That Slow Down Onset

- Eating on a full stomach
- Large, heavy meals
- Slower metabolism
- First-time or rare use
- Lower-fat meals

## What If Someone Consumes Too Much?

It happens. Here's how to help:

1. **Stay calm** — Anxiety makes everything feel worse
2. **Reassure them** — Effects are temporary and will pass
3. **Find a comfortable space** — Quiet, dim, safe
4. **Hydrate** — Water, juice, or non-caffeinated beverages
5. **Try black pepper** — Chewing peppercorns may reduce anxiety
6. **Offer CBD** — Can counteract some THC effects
7. **Wait it out** — Effects typically taper within 2-4 hours

**When to seek help:**
- Extreme panic or paranoia that doesn't subside
- Chest pain or difficulty breathing
- If the person becomes unresponsive

## Building Tolerance Awareness

If you're hosting regularly:
- Same dose may feel different on different nights
- Full stomach = slower but longer effects
- Empty stomach = faster and potentially more intense
- Alcohol intensifies THC effects (be careful)
